## H — Y-axis convention audit (TID 699141)

**Question.** Does any of our scalar engine, JAX engine, or orbit math
silently apply a `+y down` (render-style) convention while the game
observation uses `+y up` (Cartesian)?

**Method.** Grep `lib/orbit.py`, `lib/game/interpreter.py`,
`lib/game/jax/jax_interpreter.py` for `100 - y`, `invert_yaxis`,
`y_flip`, `y_invert`, `-y` sign-flips, and inverted angular-velocity
applications.

**Result.** Zero hits. `lib/orbit.py:43` and `:63` add
`angular_velocity * lead_turns` directly to `cur_angle` with no sign
inversion. No `100 - y` anywhere.

**Verdict.** Clean. The team's coordinate convention is internally
consistent with the engine.

## N(b) — Fog-of-war zeroing audit (TID 698478)

**Question.** Does our WorldModel anywhere defensively zero enemy
fleet fields (especially `angle`) on the assumption that they are
hidden? Per TID 698478, the angle of every fleet — including enemy
fleets — is fully visible in `obs.fleets`.

**Method.** Grep `lib/world_model.py` and `lib/intent.py` for any
branch keyed on `owner != my_id` that drops or masks fleet fields.

**Result.** Only legitimate filter is in `world_model.py:203`, where
`enemy_etas` is filtered to `owner != my_id` arrivals — this is
correctly separating *our* vs *their* arrivals for ETA aggregation,
not zeroing data. `lib/trajectory.py:56-57` uses `fleet.angle` via
`math.cos`/`math.sin` regardless of owner.

**Verdict.** Clean. We use enemy fleet angles directly, as the game
exposes them.

## K — Eval-environment cost probe (TID 697682)

**Question.** Will `v7_0_drop_one` time out at Kaggle eval? Eval
allocates **0.6 CPU** + **8 GB RAM** per player, with a hard
**1.0 s/turn** budget plus **60 s total overage budget**.

**Method.** Ran one full 500-step self-play episode of
`submissions/v7_0_drop_one.py` via `scripts.tournament.run_tournament`
(which records per-turn wallclock per seat). Three configurations:

1. Unconstrained (4 cores available).
2. `taskset -c 0` — pinned to a single core.
3. `taskset -c 0` while a `yes >/dev/null` burner runs pinned to the
   same core (forces a ~50 % CPU share).

| Config            | Wall   | mean   | p50   | p95    | p99    | max    | turns > 1 s | overage |
| ----------------- | -----: | -----: | ----: | -----: | -----: | -----: | ----------: | ------: |
| Unconstrained     | 64.7 s | 63.2   | 3.4   | 216.5  | 266.4  | 405.5  | 0 / 998     | 0 ms    |
| `taskset -c 0`    | 64.1 s | 62.7   | 3.4   | 215.4  | 262.5  | 427.3  | 0 / 998     | 0 ms    |
| 50 % CPU share    | 128.9 s| 126.2  | 7.5   | 426.0  | 541.0  | 815.1  | 0 / 998     | 0 ms    |

(Times in milliseconds. `N_turns = 998` is the two seats × 499 turns
of the 500-step game.)

**Observations.**

- `taskset -c 0` is identical to unconstrained → v7 is single-threaded
  (it doesn't use more than one core even when available).
- 50 % contention scales every wallclock metric by exactly 2.0×.
  Linear with CPU share, no superlinear blow-up.
- Eval's 0.6-CPU constraint is **less restrictive** than the 0.5-CPU
  share we measured. Extrapolating: p99 ≈ 266 / 0.6 ≈ 444 ms,
  max ≈ 405 / 0.6 ≈ 676 ms.
- The agent's internal wallclock budget is 700 ms
  (`lib/v7_search.choose_simple_2p(..., wallclock_ms=700)`); the
  measured p99 sits well below it even under harsh contention.

**Verdict.** **PASS.** v7_0_drop_one fits comfortably within Kaggle
eval timing. The 700 ms internal budget gives ~25 % headroom over the
observed-under-contention p99 of 541 ms. Adding ~150-200 ms of new
work per turn (e.g., a shot-validator MLP forward pass) would still
leave us inside 1 s under realistic CPU pressure.

**Caveat.** The probe only tested the live-submission bundle
(`submissions/v7_0_drop_one.py`). When variants A-F land, repeat the
contention test before bundling.
