## Diagnosis — why aggression alone doesn't help

The aggregate share-of-integral fact is the cleanest signal: v2's
median focal share (0.151) is *lower* than v1's (0.166). More launches,
less integral. The launches aren't profitable.

Hypotheses for the v1 → v2 regression on share:

1. **Cheap captures churn.** With a 30-tick hold floor, the reward
   function approves captures whose true post-capture lifetime is
   ~5 ticks before opp recaptures. We pay full combat cost
   (k − g ships consumed) for ~5 ticks × 1 production = 5 ships
   of integral. Net negative per trade.
2. **No defense (Bug 3 still unfixed).** Own planets aren't
   candidates for reinforce launches; once opp's fleets land we
   lose the planet without firing a defensive shot. v1 lost ships
   "to enemy" via combat in 5-10 turns; v2 just loses them at the
   same rate but with more captures churning underneath.
3. **No lookahead.** baseline rolls out K=10 steps; reach-frontier
   evaluates one-shot. baseline can see "if I capture P, opp will
   recapture by sending fleet F from S in N turns" and avoids the
   trade. reach-frontier can't.

## Rule 37 — axis cap reached

Two variants on the reward-function axis (v1 default + v2 floor /
gang-up) both gate-fail at 0/N. A third reward-function variant
would be the cap. The empirical reading is clear: **the per-turn
closed-form chooser axis is structurally falsified against a
K-step rollout opponent.**

What's NOT falsified:

  losers separation 0.488 stands).

  signal among many, not the sole objective).

What IS falsified:
- The reach-frontier objective as a *framework replacement* for
  the K-step rollout chooser (doctrine §6).
- Aggression alone as the remedy when the chooser silenced.
