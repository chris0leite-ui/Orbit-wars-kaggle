## Felipe gate WIN doesn't generalize

C3's value-head ship_balance term flipped Felipe-P0 (LOSS→WIN at step
174). But the same change cost 9-12 wins on the panel. The Felipe flip
is real but isolated — the modeling change doesn't compose with the
broader panel of v3.5.1 / v7_0 opponents.

## Hypothesis A — `ship_balance_weight = 0.3` is too aggressive

`evaluate_value_v12` weights `ship_balance` at 0.3. For a leaf state:
- Successful capture: ships move from my-garrison to my-new-planet
  (net ship count up by the production-stream component, minus
  combat losses)
- Bounced launch: ships die outright (net ship count down by full
  launch size)

The chooser may now over-penalize HIGH-RISK launches (where the
worst case is a full bounce). v3.5.1's aggressive snipe pipeline
relies on launching 0.7×src ships at carefully-sized targets;
v12-C3's value head might be reading those launches as "ship
balance dropping" if combat predictions in the K=10 rollout aren't
perfectly accurate.

Test: tune `ship_balance_weight` down to 0.1 or 0.05. If panel
recovers, confirms.

## Hypothesis B — CRN's "me-idle" baseline opp_traj is wrong opponent model

The `record_opp_traj` builds opp's K-step actions assuming I idle.
That gives opp the chance to expand UNOPPOSED for K turns. When I
then score a portfolio where I'm launching, the opp's trajectory
still reflects "v3.5.1 expanding against an idle me" — which is opp's
strongest scenario.

If my action is defensive/aggressive, opp's actual best response
would differ. The CRN simplification (opp's actions are fixed)
may bias the chooser's evaluation toward "actions that look good
against an opp expanding into vacuum."

Test: run v12-C2 (CRN only, original `evaluate_value`) vs v3.5.1 on
4-seed panel. If still bad, CRN is the regression.

## Hypothesis C — Lib drift

Parity check vs `submissions/v4_planner.py` was 0 mismatches on 3
seeds × 20 turns. That's narrow. The libs may diverge later in games
or on uncommon code paths. If `agents/v12` with `evaluate_value`
(original, NOT v12) also regresses panel, the lib drift is the
culprit.

Test: temporarily switch `agents/v12/main.py` to use original
`evaluate_value`, panel-eval. If still bad, lib drift.

## Hypothesis D — v4_planner-bundle ALSO has this problem

The bundle scored μ=1056 on the live ladder, but its head-to-head
performance vs specific top heuristics like v3.5.1 may be similarly
weak. The ladder's TrueSkill is multi-opp; the panel measures
specific head-to-heads.

Test: panel v4_planner-bundle (via `--agent v4_planner` short-name)
vs same 3 opps. If v4_planner-bundle also gets 1/16 vs v7_0 and
3/16 vs v3.5.1, the regression is the architecture, NOT C3.

## What this tells us about the methodology

The "look at games to improve modeling" loop worked at the GAME level
(Felipe forensic → ship-balance hypothesis → Felipe flip). But it did
NOT verify that the fix composed with the broader panel. A
single-seed win doesn't generalize without a panel gate.

The plan's verification step Gate 5 (panel) is what's catching this
correctly. Don't ship before the panel is green.
