## Diagnosis — what we know vs. what we suspect

**Known:** 8/8 vs `random` (Wilson 0.68-1.00), 0/8 vs `nearest` (Wilson
0.00-0.32), 0/20 vs `agents/baseline`. The chooser beats the absolute
floor (random) but is dominated by every heuristic above it. Per-turn
moves average ~1.04 (single-launch dominant) even when 5+ sources are
available; the Hungarian almost always picks one positive-value column
and routes the rest to noop.

**Hypothesised failure modes** (in rough order of "most likely":

1. **Reward function is too conservative.** With λ_loss = 0.1, even
   the cheapest captures yield small or negative reward in early /
   mid game because expected garrison saturates the integral gain.
   The "no-launch" noop slot at cost 0 wins against most positive
   columns. Even with a more aggressive λ_loss this didn't dramatically
   improve the win-rate.

2. **ρ_opp over-states opp's reach.** We use
   `WorldModel.time_to_enemy_threat` which enumerates ALL opponent
   sources, picks the minimum, and adds a source-recovery cost
   driven by the opp's STRONGEST source production. In practice
   opp must choose between competing demands and won't actually
   defend every target. With over-estimated ρ_opp, our hold_time
   shrinks → reward shrinks → we skip many captures the baseline
   profitably takes.

3. **No reinforce / defense layer.** baseline ships REINFORCE_EMIT,
   SNIPER, COMBAT_STACK, STAGNANT_DRAIN modes (all gated by env var,
   most default OFF). The reach-frontier chooser is a flat "fire-now
   from each source at most once" model with no multi-source
   coordination at a single target ("gang-up") and no reinforce.
   doctrine §6 said defend would fall out of the same objective via
   the lp.py noop slot. Empirically it doesn't — the noop slot just
   means "do nothing this turn." baseline's reinforce / stack /
   sniper modes do actual planet-saving / target-piling that the
   reach-frontier objective doesn't express.

4. **No multi-turn planning / commitment.** doctrine §1 explicitly
   deferred multi-turn LP and commitment ledger to v2. baseline's
   `chooser_trajectory` (default) does a K-step rollout so its
   choices are coherent across turns. Reach-frontier re-evaluates
   per turn from current obs and may oscillate (chase / abandon /
   re-chase) when the noisy mid-game shifts ρ_me / ρ_opp.

5. **The 2P-only optimization.** doctrine §8 / design §1 explicitly
   ship 2P first and accept "4P kingmaker" risk. This eval was 2P
   self-play, so 4P isn't the issue here. But the chooser may still
   be implicitly 4P-shaped in ways we don't see — e.g. the
   "strongest-opp production" recovery cost is conservative for 4P
   (one of three) and over-restrictive for 2P (the one opponent
   IS the strongest).

## Rule 4 — three untried mechanisms

1. **Replace ρ_opp's max-min assumption with a softer probabilistic
   model.** Instead of "opp reaches every target with their best
   source," treat each opp source as having a budget; sum-of-claims
   across opp targets must not exceed opp's total ships. Solve a
   small LP from opp's side too, then read ρ_opp as the min cost
   that the opp's LP actually commits. This makes opp's defense
   realistic and our hold_time longer on average. Implementation
   cost: ~80 LOC; mirrors our own assignment but for opp.

2. **Gang-up via stacking columns.** Allow up to K sources to send
   fleets at a single target in one turn. lp.py already supports
   per-target gang-up cap (`DEFAULT_MAX_CONTESTERS_PER_TARGET = 3`)
   in `solve_multi_turn`; just need to wire reach-frontier through
   that solver instead of the simpler `build_assignment_matrix`.
   Implementation cost: ~30 LOC.

3. **Augment, don't replace.** Stack reach-frontier as an additional
   layer over baseline's chooser_trajectory: baseline picks its
   move set, then reach-frontier adds any of ITS positive-reward
   launches that don't conflict (distinct source AND target).
   Trade-off: this violates doctrine §6 ("replace, don't stack").
   But empirically baseline already wins games; reach-frontier's
   contribution is just net-additional captures the rollout missed.
   Implementation cost: ~40 LOC.

## Recommendation to PI

Per the plan's Part C stop conditions and Rule 1 (no submit without
PI sign-off), the build is **paused**. Three options:

- **(A) Iterate on reach-frontier in isolation** — pursue (1)
  probabilistic ρ_opp or (2) gang-up stacking. ~2-4 h work per
  variant; gate at B5 vs baseline before considering submit. Rule 37
  caps at 3 variants on the same axis.
- **(B) Pivot to the stack-over-baseline approach** (3 above) —
  violates doctrine §6 but empirically the lowest-risk path to a
  positive A/B vs baseline. PI sign-off needed because it
  re-architects the doctrine's "replacement, not augmentation"
  premise.
- **(C) Park the chooser and continue the audit / mechanism work
  on baseline** — the doctrine, empirical study, and design docs
  remain in tree as durable reference. Closed track per Rule 4 only
  if the PI judges this an exhausted axis.

The empirical study from earlier (audit/2026-05-27-hold-time-
empirical.md) showed share-of-integral discriminates winners from
losers; doctrine math is sound. What this v1 attempt proves is the
operationalisation gap: closed-form per-turn ρ + Hungarian is not
sufficient to extract the integral against an opponent who plans
ahead. doctrine §1's "v2 axes" (commitment ledger, multi-turn LP,
opp model learning) may be load-bearing, not optional.
