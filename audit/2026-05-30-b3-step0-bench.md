## TL;DR — substrate WORKS; cost model in B.3 plan is wrong

| Bench | Verdict | What it tells us |
|---|---|---|
| **V0.0** pv_eta determinism on identical obs | **PASS** | 3/3 calls identical at wc=100ms. CRN cancellation assumption holds at this snap. |
| **V0.1** fast_sim rollout determinism (with reset) | **PASS** | 2 rollouts on same snap → identical totals. No state leakage signal even without reset between rollouts in this state. |
| **V0.2** fast_sim K=10 rollout p95 < 100ms | **FAIL** | p50 **2021 ms**, p95 **2139 ms**. The <100 ms target was based on an incorrect cost model — see analysis below. |
| **V0.3** fast_sim vs env.step parity (action-replay) | **PASS** | 10/10 seeds match at integer precision on terminal `ship_totals`. fast_sim.step is byte-equivalent to env.step for pv_eta-shaped actions. |

**Overall**: fast_sim is **correct** and **deterministic** as a B.3 substrate (V0.0, V0.1, V0.3 all clean). The V0.2 timing FAIL is real but **does not push us off any cliff**, because the B.3 plan's 7.5h-vs-150h dichotomy rested on a substrate-cost model that the measurements falsify (below).

## Why V0.2 failed — and why it doesn't matter

The B.3 plan author estimated K=10 pv_eta-vs-pv_eta rollouts at "20-50 ms" based on `lib/fast_sim.py`'s claimed ~20× speedup vs `env.clone+step`. **That speedup is real, but it only applies to env.step compute** — it does not speed up the policy callable. Component breakdown at the warm seed=42 snap:

| Component | Cost | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Single `pv_eta.agent(obs, cfg)` call at wc=100 ms | **65-66 ms** | Iterative deepening; uses ~⅔ of wallclock budget |
| fast_sim K=10 with **noop** policies | **0.6 ms** | Substrate-only cost; ~0.06 ms per step |
| `env.clone()` alone | **22 ms** | Per-rollout env copy overhead |
| 10 × `env.step([], [])` (noop) on cloned env | **17 ms** | env's per-step overhead |
| **fast_sim K=10 with [pv_eta, pv_eta]** | **2021 ms** | 20 pv_eta calls dominate; substrate is rounding error |
| **env.clone + K=10 with [pv_eta, pv_eta]** | **1469 ms** | Same 20 pv_eta calls; **measured ~25% FASTER than fast_sim path** |

pv_eta consumes ~95-100% of per-rollout wallclock. The substrate choice is irrelevant to total cost. Worse: fast_sim measured *slower* than env.clone+step in this configuration (open question — possibly pv_eta's `_as_dict(Struct)` coercion or denser state after fast_sim ticks; not chased because it doesn't change the verdict).

## Updated B.3 stage-2 cost model

Per-rollout cost at the measured per-call rate × 2 seats × K ticks (env.clone+step path; substrate-independent):

| top-N | K | games | wc (ms) | rollouts | per-roll | single-core | 4-core parallel |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 10 | 10 | 100 | 100 | ~165 000 | 1.5 s | **~69 h** | ~17 h |
| 5  | 10 | 100 | 100 |  ~90 000 | 1.5 s | **~37 h** |  ~9 h |
| 5  | 10 |  50 | 100 |  ~45 000 | 1.5 s | **~19 h** |  ~5 h |
| 5  |  5 |  50 | 100 |  ~45 000 | 0.75 s | **~9.4 h** |  ~2.4 h |
| 5  |  5 |  50 |  25 |  ~45 000 | 0.5 s | **~6.3 h** |  ~1.6 h |

(Assumes ~150 states per game and that the per-state "idle" rollout is cached/shared across all top-N candidates of that state. Without that caching, costs ~2× higher.)

**Implications**:

- The 7.5 h "best case" in `audit/2026-05-30-reframe-b3-crn-advantage-plan.md` is achievable only at aggressive knob reduction (top-N=5, K=5, ~50 games, low wallclock).
- The 150 h "fallback" is the **realistic** cost only at the maximal knobs (top-N=10, K=10, 100 games, wc=100). The fallback wasn't due to substrate — it was due to corpus size.
- fast_sim being correct does NOT unlock a 20× speedup over the fallback; **it does not help at all** for this use case because pv_eta dominates.

## What this changes about the B.3 plan

1. **`lib/fast_sim_pv_eta.py` is unnecessary as an optimization.** It also measured slightly slower than the env.clone+step alternative. Drop it from the file-change list; have `scripts/compute_crn_advantage.py` use `env.clone()` + `env.step()` directly.
2. **The cost-knob discussion is now the primary design question, not "does fast_sim work."** Cost scales multiplicatively in `pv_eta_wallclock_ms × 2 × K × N_rollouts`.
3. **The "open question" set for PI gets a new entry**: what `BASELINE_WALLCLOCK_MS` should pv_eta use as policy in stage 2? Tradeoff: lower wc → faster corpus, but labels reflect a weaker policy → labels less indicative of the *strong* pv_eta we're actually deploying. (Default suggestion: keep wc=100 ms — same as the bundle's standard A/B harness setting — so labels match deployment behavior.)
4. **idle-rollout sharing across top-N candidates is now a load-bearing optimization**, not nice-to-have. Without it, costs ~2× higher.

## Determinism note (V0.0 PASS, important for CRN)

pv_eta is **deterministic on identical obs at wc=100 ms** in our test (step 20, seed 42). This is essential for CRN-paired advantage: `margin(action) − margin(idle)` only cancels noise if both rollouts use the same (deterministic) opponent policy. The bundled pv_eta uses iterative deepening with a wallclock budget but appears to converge to the same actions across calls — likely because at this snap (early game, 6 planets, sparse fleets) search completes well before the 100 ms budget.

**Caveat**: at later game states (denser fleet sets, more candidates), pv_eta may hit its wallclock budget and the action-equivalence may break. A follow-up determinism check on a denser state (e.g. step 100 with full fleet density) is worth running before kicking off the full stage-2 corpus, since CRN's noise-cancellation property is load-bearing for B.3.

## Decision

The right next move is **not** "proceed with B.3 stage 1 + 2 as planned." It's:

1. **Re-engage with PI on the 5 open design questions** in `audit/2026-05-30-reframe-b3-crn-advantage-plan.md` armed with the new cost model. The "top-N" and "K" questions especially are now revealed to be multiplicative cost knobs, not strategy refinements.
2. **Drop `lib/fast_sim_pv_eta.py` from the file-change list.** The labelling driver `scripts/compute_crn_advantage.py` should use `env.clone()` directly.
3. **Add idle-rollout caching as a mandatory feature** of the labelling driver.
4. **Add a wallclock-determinism follow-up bench** at a denser game state before kicking off the full stage-2 run.

**Compute claim**: B.3 stage 2 is feasible at moderate knobs (~6-10 h single-core; ~2 h on 4 cores or Kaggle 4×notebooks). It is NOT feasible at the maximal knobs (top-N=10, K=10, 100 games, wc=100 ⇒ ~70 h single-core).
