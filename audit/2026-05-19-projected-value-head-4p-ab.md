## Verdict: production-compounding unification (`max` aggregation) FALSIFIED

The exact piece I was most confident about — `max` over opponents — is
the cause. Predicted-vs-observed:

- **Predicted (plan, 2026-05-19):** `max` picks the runaway leader; if
  opp1 and opp2 fight each other, our cost shrinks for free. Should
  outperform favor's `sum_of_opps` in 4P.
- **Observed:** projected lost 8.6 pp to favor in the same 4P panel.

The math was right in the limit. The realised game falsifies the
assumption that only-the-leader-matters. **Every opponent is a threat at
every step.** By only weighing the leader, projected stops defending
against the 2nd and 3rd-place opps — they capture our weak planets and
we don't react until they become the leader, by which point the lead
is gone.

Favor's `sum_of_opps` aggregation in 4P is doing real work: it's the
pessimistic baseline that forces the chooser to push back against all
three opponents. Removing it broke 4P play.

## What this falsifies and what it does not

**Falsified:** the specific `max`-aggregator form of
`projected_rank_diff` in `lib/value_heads.py:VALUE_HEAD_CHOICE = 2`.
This implementation is a 4P regression.

**Not yet falsified:** the broader claim that a per-seat ProjectedTotal
scalar can replace favor's split F1+F2. We've only tested `max`
aggregation. A `sum`-aggregated variant would distinguish "aggregator
was wrong" from "per-seat-projection adds no value over favor."
